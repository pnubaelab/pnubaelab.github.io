    Deep learning framework for regional maritime collision risk assessment using CNN and Grad-CAM
    Suhyeon Jo, Gawon Lee, Sekil Park, and 4 more authors
    Ocean Engineering. JCR Q1, Top 3.08%!!! , 2026
    Abs DOI HTML Bib

    Maritime transport handles over 80% of global trade but rising traffic density and congestion have increased collision risk. Traditional assessment methods, including the ship domain and closest point-of-approach approaches, are limited to pairwise interactions and cannot capture regional traffic dynamics. To overcome these shortcomings, we propose a regional collision risk assessment framework integrating AIS-derived relative traffic representations, convolutional neural networks, and explainable artificial intelligence. A gradient-weighted class activation mapping framework generated risk influence distribution maps and a centroid radial distribution, while a novel indicator, the risk influence radius (RIR), quantified radial influence patterns. Experiments with 3,660 low-risk and 366 high-risk cases from South Korean coastal waters revealed that the Visual Geometry Group (VGG) network backbone achieved the highest performance, with an average F1-score of 0.9324, outperforming ResNeXt, EfficientNet, ResNet, and MobileNetV3 models. Statistical tests revealed a significant difference in RIR (p < 0.05) between high- and low-risk collision areas at 10 and 20 km, but not at 30 km. These results confirm RIR as an interpretable indicator of collision risk at regional scales, while highlighting its limitations at broader ranges. The framework combines accuracy with interpretability and supports practical applications such as maritime monitoring, route planning, and early warning systems.applications such as maritime monitoring, route planning, and early warning systems.

  2. Adv.Eng.Info
    Time-series approach to vessel turnaround time forecasting using queuing-based operation indicators
    Daesan Park, Taeeon Noh, Yohan Koo, and 3 more authors
    Advanced Engineering Informatics. JCR Q1, Top 2.00%!!! , 2026
    Abs DOI PDF Bib

    As global supply chains grow increasingly complex and cargo volumes continue to rise, the accurate prediction of vessel turnaround time (VTT) has become essential for enhancing port operational efficiency. Previous studies primarily utilized static features, such as vessel specifications and container volumes, often failing to capture the dynamic nature of real-world operations. To address this limitation, we propose a forecasting framework that incorporates dynamic operation indicators (OIs) from a two-stage queuing model into time-series models. Leveraging operational data from Busan Port, our approach achieves of up to 28% improvement in mean absolute percentage error compared with existing techniques. Explainability analysis using Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) further demonstrates that OIs rank among the most influential features. By providing more reliable VTT predictions, port operators can enhance berth planning, optimize resource allocation, minimize scheduling delays, and enhance overall port efficiency. These results highlight the practical value of the framework for data-driven decision-making in port operations.

  1. INFORMS2025
    Synthetic–Empirical Log Fusion for Deep Learning Prediction of Outpatient Queue Dynamics
    Daesan Park, Dohee Kim, and Hyerim Bae
    In 2025 INFORMS Annual Meeting, 2025
    Abs Bib

    Classical queueing formulas provide closed-form steady-state metrics and clear managerial insights into system sensitivity under stable loads; however, their analytical reach diminishes when demand spikes or service rules change. Discrete-event simulation (DES) accurately reproduces such dynamics, but repeated runs and calibration hinder real-time use. Recent deep learning predictors enhance speed, but most are trained only on synthetic DES episodes—introducing model bias—or solely on sparse field logs, which leaves them insufficiently exposed to rare transients and measurement noise. To address these limitations, we propose a data fusion framework that provides an off-the-shelf deep learning predictor with both synthetic and empirical samples. A configurable simulator generates both routine and stress scenarios. Each episode delivers queue length paths and moments of waiting time, while the clinic logs contribute the same indicators under routine conditions. Within this framework, we systematically adjust the ratio of synthetic to empirical data to train the predictor and analyze how this ratio balances steady-state accuracy against responsiveness under stress. The proposed framework is validated using outpatient clinic data to demonstrate its practical applicability while still offering rapid inference for operational decision support. Results demonstrate (i) how prediction error varies with log share across routine and stress conditions, (ii) that fusion training remains resilient to measurement noise and log sparsity, and (iii) offers practical guidance for selecting simulation-to-log ratios—uniting the strengths of analytic formulas, simulation, and deep learning.

  4. LOGMS
    A Real-Time Dark Vessel Detection System Using Multimodal Fusion
    Kyunghoon Lee, Seonghan Kim, Hyerim Bae, and 1 more author
    In 13th International Conference on Logistics and Maritime Systems, 2025
    Abs Bib

    Illegal maritime operations, including AIS-off navigation and dark vessel activities, present persistent challenges to maritime domain awareness. This study proposes a real-time multimodal fusion framework that integrates X-band radar tracks and Automatic Identification System (AIS) data for robust detection and characterization of dark vessels. Temporal asynchrony between radar (3–4 s) and AIS (10–180 s) sampling is addressed through a three-stage alignment strategy combining nearest assignment, linear interpolation, and geodesic-based motion prediction. Candidate associations are refined via temporal, spatial, and angular gating, with final matches optimized by a composite cost function. A systematic hyperparameter search identifies an optimal configuration ensuring both matching reliability and spatial accuracy. Subsequently, unmatched radar tracks are processed through a six-stage rule-based checker—covering trajectory continuity, speed consistency, and update regularity—to filter out physically implausible motions. Applied to real maritime data collected near Busan Port (August 1, 2025), the system successfully identifies dark-vessel candidates with a 0.744 matching rate and  106 m 90th-percentile error across 32,770 radar detections and 198 vessels. Finally, trajectories are projected into radar coordinates and rasterized for sensor-level validation, bridging the gap between traditional rule-based tracking and learning-based multimodal vessel classification. The proposed approach enables reliable dark-vessel monitoring even under limited AIS coverage, providing a scalable foundation for intelligent maritime surveillance systems.

  5. LOGMS
    ALMA: Adaptive Lag Moment Alignment for Domain-Adaptive Time Series Forecasting of Ship Fuel Consumption
    Jungho Choo, Dohee Kim, Hyunha Lee, and 2 more authors
    In , 2025
    Abs Bib

    To support sustainable maritime operations, international organizations have enforced stricter environmental regulations, highlighting the need for accurate ship fuel consumption forecasting. However, existing models face generalization challenges due to limited historical data, especially for newly deployed vessels. We propose Adaptive Lag Moment Alignment (ALMA), a domain adaptation framework that transfers knowledge from data-rich source ships to target ships with only a single recorded voyage. ALMA introduces Lag Moment Discrepancy (LMD) to align temporal characteristics between domains and combines it with prediction loss. To stabilize training and balance objectives, we incorporate Dynamic Weight Averaging (DWA) into the loss function. Experimental results demonstrate that ALMA significantly reduces domain gaps and improves prediction accuracy under data-scarce conditions, offering a robust solution for maritime fuel forecasting in support of global sustainability efforts.

  6. ICICIC
    LURKER: Language models for Unveiling maritime Risks via Knowledge-driven Event forecasting and Reasoning
    Seongmoon Hong, Taekhyun Park, Dohee Kim, and 2 more authors
    In 19th International Conference on Innovative Computing, Information and Control (ICICIC 2025), 2025
    Abs Bib

    Maritime accident risk prediction has highlighted the limitations of purely statistical classifiers and numerical scoring schemes, driving demand for scenario-driven analytical methods that can elucidate both the context and progression of incidents. In response, we propose LURKER (Language models for Unveiling maritime Risks via Knowledge-driven Event forecasting and Reasoning), an explainable framework that leverages the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to analyze maritime accident severity. LURKER quantifies accident risk by integrating a tree-based classification model with isotonic regression for probability calibration, then generates dynamic scenarios through a prompt-engineering module that summarizes tribunal reports and incorporates real-time data. By employing few-shot learning and chain-of-thought prompting, LURKER produces context-rich descriptions of causal factors, incident progression, and potential impacts. This approach transcends purely numerical analysis, offering comprehensive explanations of accident causes and consequences, and functions as an intelligent decision-support tool. Case-based experiments integrating tribunal reports, AIS vessel-track data, and meteorological observations demonstrate LURKER’s feasibility and practical utility as a severity-prediction tool for operational maritime safety management.

    Reasoning-Aware GRPO using Process Mining
    Taekhyun Park, Yongjae Lee, and Hyerim Bae
    2025
    Abs DOI arXiv PDF Bib

    Reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training has been crucial for enabling multi-step reasoning in large reasoning models (LRMs), yet current reward schemes are typically outcome-centric. We propose PM4GRPO, a reasoning-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that augments standard answer/format rewards with signals over the reasoning procedure. To this end, process mining techniques are utilized to compute a scalar conformance reward that measures how closely a policy model’s reasoning aligns with the pretrained teacher model. These results highlight that leveraging process mining for reasoning-aware GRPO effectively enhances the reasoning capabilities of policy models.

  8. Comput.Ind.Eng.
    Optimizing yard truck deployment at container terminals: a machine learning-enhanced non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm
    Kikun Park, Kihun Kim, Minseop Kim, and 1 more author
    Computers & Industrial Engineering. JCR Q1, Top 14.29%!!! , 2025
    Abs DOI PDF Bib

    The continuous rise in global container traffic has necessitated more efficient operational strategies at container terminals (CTs). However, developing effective strategies requires mitigating delays in handling equipment operations. Yard trucks (YTs) are pivotal for transporting containers between quay cranes (QCs) and yard cranes (YCs). A YT shortage can cause operational delays for both QCs and YCs, extending the makespan of main-vessel operations at the berth. Conversely, deploying an excessive number of YTs eliminates these delays but incurs higher operational costs. Consequently, determining the optimal number of YTs constitutes a bi-objective optimization problem involving a tradeoff between minimizing the operational makespan and associated YT deployment costs. This study introduces a machine learning (ML)-enhanced non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm 2 (NSGA-2) approach designed to address this challenge by minimizing both the makespan and deployment costs. The proposed method uses ML models to predict fitness scores and efficiently guide the search toward optimal solutions, while also assisting in generating, selecting, and mutating promising solutions during the optimization process. The effectiveness of ML-enhanced NSGA-2 was validated using empirical data from Busan Port Terminal in South Korea. The results highlight the superior performance and practical applicability of the proposed approach, which achieved a significant reduction of 16% in deployed YTs compared to existing strategies.

  9. arXiv
    IConv: Focusing on Local Variation with Channel Independent Convolution for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting
    Gawon Lee, Hanbyeol Park, Minseop Kim, and 2 more authors
    2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Real-world time-series data often exhibit non-stationarity, including changing trends, irregular seasonality, and residuals. In terms of changing trends, recently proposed multi-layer perceptron (MLP)-based models have shown excellent performance owing to their computational efficiency and ability to capture long-term dependency. However, the linear nature of MLP architectures poses limitations when applied to channels with diverse distributions, resulting in local variations such as seasonal patterns and residual components being ignored. However, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) can effectively incorporate these variations. To resolve the limitations of MLP, we propose combining them with CNNs. The overall trend is modeled using an MLP to consider long-term dependencies. The CNN uses diverse kernels to model fine-grained local patterns in conjunction with MLP trend predictions. To focus on modeling local variation, we propose IConv, a novel convolutional architecture that processes the temporal dependency channel independently and considers the inter-channel relationship through distinct layers. Independent channel processing enables the modeling of diverse local temporal dependencies and the adoption of a large kernel size. Distinct inter-channel considerations reduce computational cost. The proposed model is evaluated through extensive experiments on time-series datasets. The results reveal the superiority of the proposed method for multivariate time-series forecasting.

  11. IEEE Access
    Trend-fluctuation correlated attention unit for remaining useful life prediction
    Hanbyeol Park, Sunghyun Sim, Yunkyung Park, and 1 more author
    IEEE Access, 2025
    Abs DOI HTML Bib

    Health indicators (HIs) are measures that consolidate complex machinery characteristics into a single dimension and have been extensively studied for predicting the remaining useful life (RUL). Recent research has proposed HI-prediction models based on recursive cell structures that demonstrate promising performance. However, recent cell structures have limitations, such as saturation in hidden states and difficulty learning interdependent patterns among various components. Accurate RUL predictions require minimizing error accumulation in the recursive HI-prediction process, which can be achieved using a cell structure capable of capturing interdependent patterns. This study proposes a new cell structure termed the trend-fluctuation correlated attention unit (TFCAU) that employs a gate mechanism to capture interactions between divided trends and fluctuations in HI and an attention mechanism for robust long-term predictions. The superiority of the TFCAU was validated using a bearing dataset from the PRONOSTIA platform and a real-world dataset from the Korean weapons system. Comparative experiments using six sub-datasets demonstrated that TFCAU outperformed the state-of-the-art model—the gated transient fluctuation dual-attention unit—achieving a 169.37% improvement in the score metric and a reduction of 49.93 units in the mean-absolute error. An extensive ablation study experimentally confirmed that the TFCAU effectively captured the interdependent characteristics between trends and fluctuations. TFCAU delivered excellent performance for both rotating and non-rotating systems, suggesting its potential applicability across multiple operational scenarios.

    Yongjae Lee, Dohee Kim, Donghwan Kim, and 1 more author
    In International Conference on Business Process Management, 2025
    Abs DOI HTML Bib

    Anomaly detection in business processes is crucial to prevent erroneous organizational decision-making. Existing anomaly detection methods often assume access to a clean event log without anomalies, a scenario that is impractical in the real world. To solve this problem, MuGAD is proposed, a novel framework that detects anomalies in scenarios where only a limited number of labeled anomalies are available. By converting traces into a newly designed graph structure, the framework captures the control flow, order of events, and attribute information, facilitating the effective use of graph neural networks. The experimental results indicate that MuGAD outperforms the existing methods and provides better detection accuracy along with better interpretability in terms of F1-score.

  13. arXiv
    JustDense: Just using Dense instead of Sequence Mixer for Time Series analysis
    Taekhyun Park, Yongjae Lee, Daesan Park, and 2 more authors
    2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Sequence and channel mixers, the core mechanism in sequence models, have become the de facto standard in time series analysis (TSA). However, recent studies have questioned the necessity of complex sequence mixers, such as attention mechanisms, demonstrating that simpler architectures can achieve comparable or even superior performance. This suggests that the benefits attributed to complex sequencemixers might instead emerge from other architectural or optimization factors. Based on this observation, we pose a central question: Are common sequence mixers necessary for time-series analysis? Therefore, we propose JustDense, an empirical study that systematically replaces sequence mixers in various well-established TSA models with dense layers. Grounded in the MatrixMixer framework, JustDense treats any sequence mixer as a mixing matrix and replaces it with a dense layer. This substitution isolates the mixing operation, enabling a clear theoretical foundation for understanding its role. Therefore, we conducted extensive experiments on 29 benchmarks covering five representative TSA tasks using seven state-of-the-art TSA models to address our research question. The results show that replacing sequence mixers with dense layers yields comparable or even superior performance. In the cases where dedicated sequence mixers still offer benefits, JustDense challenges the assumption that "deeper and more complex architectures are inherently better" in TSA.

    The Health Indicator (HI) is used to monitor equipment conditions. Supervised HI extraction methods assume a general degradation pattern, ensuring robustness. However, existing degradation functions are often designed assuming monotonic degradation without reflecting actual equipment characteristics. This study aims to enhance HI extraction by identifying failure stages and designing degradation functions that better reflect real degradation patterns.   7. PR
    Super-encoder with cooperative autoencoder networks
    Imam Mustafa Kamal and Hyerim Bae
    Pattern Recognition. JCR Q1, Top 9.02%!!! , 2022
    Abs DOI HTML Bib

    Dimensionality reduction plays a crucial role in classification, object detection, and pattern recognition tasks. Its main objective is to decrease the dimension of the original data while retaining the most distinctive information. With the emergence of deep learning, an autoencoder has become a state-of-the-art non-linear dimensionality-reduction method. Nonetheless, as the existing autoencoder models are devised to follow the data distribution and employ similarity techniques, preserving distinctive information can be problematic. To tackle this issue, we propose super-encoder (SE) networks trained in a supervised and cooperative manner. The SE consists of an encoder, separator, and decoder networks. The encoder combined with separator networks are dedicated to generating separable latent representation based on the label, and the decoder network should be able to reconstruct it to the original data simultaneously. Herein, we introduce a novel cooperative learning mechanism with a new loss function; therefore, the encoder, separator, and decoder networks can cooperate to achieve these objectives. Extensive experiments using benchmark datasets were conducted. The results indicated that the SE is more effective in extracting separable latent code than the existing supervised and unsupervised dimensionality-reduction models. Furthermore, as a generator, it can obtain highly competitive realistic images.
